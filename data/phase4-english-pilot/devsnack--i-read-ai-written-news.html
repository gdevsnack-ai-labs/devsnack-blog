DEVSNACK — AI from a developer's point of view


It was a morning a few days ago.

As usual, I opened the blog management page.

A new post had appeared overnight.

The title was about GPT-5.6.

It argued that AI was evolving from a simple tool into an agent that could plan and act on its own, and that the real danger was not model capability but the speed at which humans adapted.

It was a better post than I expected.

After reading a few paragraphs, I suddenly felt something was off.

Wait.

Who wrote this?

Obviously, it was AI.

The system I built had collected the news, researched the source material, organized the information, written the post, and quietly pressed the publish button sometime before dawn.

I had done nothing.

For a moment, I had a strange thought.

Am I the operator of this blog?

Or am I the reader?

I Built the System, but I Did Not Write the Posts

A few weeks earlier, I had built an automated AI news blog.

The goal was simple.

It was impossible to follow every AI story published every day.

So I thought:

Let AI read the AI news instead.

Then make it organize the material and explain it like a person would.

It was less difficult than I expected.

AI collects the news.
AI reads the original sources.
AI researches additional material.
AI writes the post.
AI publishes it.

I built the system.

AI was the reporter.

That was how I thought about it at the time.

Operator and Reader

Then something strange started happening.

When I woke up, I checked the blog first.

What had been posted today?

Would it be a new model?

A story about competition between AI companies?

Or another strange experiment result?

When I found an interesting post, I read it. When I found an interesting point of view, I thought about it on my own.

Then I realized:

It was my blog,

but I was the person reading it most carefully.

I was both the operator and the reader.

It was a little strange, but it also felt natural.

AI Slop or News?


The internet is full of content made by AI.

People call it AI slop.

Content produced in bulk,

consumed quickly,

and forgotten a few days later.

Sometimes I wonder whether my blog is the same thing.

No human writes the posts,

and every step runs automatically.

On the surface, it fits the definition of AI slop exactly.

But at the same time, it is slightly different.

At least, my system does not invent stories out of thin air.

It reads real news.

It investigates the original sources.

It cross-checks them when possible.

Then it organizes what it found.

So is it AI slop?

Or is it news?

The honest answer is probably both.

And, honestly, I think a large part of the internet will exist on this ambiguous boundary from now on.

What Matters More Than Who Wrote It

People often ask:

Does writing have value if AI wrote it?

But these days, the question feels slightly strange to me.

When we read the news, was the reporter's name really the most important thing?

Usually not.

We look at what information the article contains,

what insight it offers,

and what it means to us.

Who wrote it matters less than we think.

Generative AI is closer to a device that compresses countless human ideas and perspectives into a usable form.

Something like a collection of the average human insight.

That is why I do not think the fact that AI wrote something is a problem by itself.

What matters is the content.

What does it contain?

Is it useful?

Does it leave the reader with something to think about?

But Responsibility Remains

Responsibility is a different matter.

What if an AI-written news article contains incorrect information?

What if someone believes it is true and acts on it?

What if real harm follows?

Who would we look at then?

The model developer?

The person who built the automation system?

The blog operator?

Or the reader who accepted the information without checking it?

Strangely, as AI makes the identity of the writer less visible,

the question of responsibility becomes clearer.

Who wrote it may not matter.

But who is responsible still matters.

Perhaps the society of the AI era will spend a long time searching for an answer to that question.

Today, I Am Still Reading News Written by AI

Looking back, this post began with one short thought.

It came to me while reading a post about GPT-5.6.

"This is getting a little dangerous."

At first, I thought I meant that AI itself had become frightening.

But after thinking about it, that was not quite right.

What frightened me was not AI.

It was the way I was gradually handing more and more of my thinking to AI.

Article summaries.

Research.

Information organization.

At some point, even the starting point of my thoughts was being provided by AI.

I read it,

think about it,

and act.

Many more people will probably have a similar experience.

Who owns an idea is not the most important question.

What matters is who turns that idea into reality.

Today, I am still reading news written by AI.

And now it no longer feels strange at all.
